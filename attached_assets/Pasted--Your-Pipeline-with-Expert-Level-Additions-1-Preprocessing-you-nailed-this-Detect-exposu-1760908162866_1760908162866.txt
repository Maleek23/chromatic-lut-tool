✅ Your Pipeline (with Expert-Level Additions)

1. Preprocessing (you nailed this)
	•	✅ Detect exposure & white balance
	•	✅ Convert to linear scene-referred (ACEScg)
	•	✅ Denoise

🔧 Additions:
	•	Camera Response Curve Estimation – approximate the input’s camera sensor profile (you can use IT8 targets or EXIF hints).
→ ensures consistency across DSLR, phone, or cinema stills.
	•	Dynamic Range Normalization – match histogram dynamic range to 0.1–99.9 percentile (prevents crushed blacks/whites).

⸻

2. Advanced Feature Extraction
	•	✅ Dense histograms
	•	✅ K-means clustering per hue
	•	✅ Luminance-based tone curves
	•	✅ Skin tone protection

🔧 Additions:
	•	Saturation–Luminance coupling analysis – human color perception links these two, so maintaining perceived brightness while shifting hues prevents color shifts from feeling “off.”
	•	Scene context detection – detect if it’s portrait / landscape / low light / high key. Apply scene-appropriate weighting (can be lightweight ML, or rule-based).

⸻

3. Perceptual Color Space
	•	✅ Lab / LCh — perfect.

🔧 Additions:
	•	CAM16 / JzAzBz color spaces (if you want next-gen precision).
These are more modern than Lab and avoid hue skew under extreme saturations.
	•	colorjs.io supports CAM16
	•	JzAzBz is becoming the HDR grading standard (Dolby Vision uses this internally).

⸻

4. Filmic Tone Curves
	•	✅ S-curve with shoulder/toe
	•	✅ Nonlinear contrast

🔧 Additions:
	•	Adaptive S-curve – curve strength scales with scene contrast.
(If histogram is already high contrast → apply gentler curve.)
	•	Highlight roll-off tuning – emulate specific film stocks (Kodak 2383, Fuji Eterna, etc.).
You can parameterize this for your Creator or Studio tiers.

⸻

5. Gamut Mapping
	•	✅ ACES/Rec.2020 → Rec.709
	•	✅ Prevent clipping

🔧 Additions:
	•	Perceptual gamut compression (not linear clipping).
Use simple tone-mapping operators like Reinhard or Hable (Unreal) tone maps for realism.
	•	Color Appearance Preserving Mapping – convert using hue-anchored compression (avoid hue shifts when desaturating).

⸻

6. Optional ML Enhancement (Pro/Studio Tier)
	•	✅ Neural network trained on colorist grades

🔧 Additions:
	•	Style clustering – use embeddings (CLIP or ViT) to tag reference styles (“Moody teal-orange”, “Cinematic neutral”, “Natural daylight”).
→ lets users name and save their LUTs by “style,” not just file.
	•	Cross-camera LUT calibration – ML model can learn how to map one camera color science (e.g., Sony → ARRI look).
This feature alone could become a premium standalone product.

⸻

7. (Optional) Output Validation / QA
	•	Render sample swatches and compute:
	•	ΔE2000 (color difference metric)
	•	Tone curve consistency
	•	Clipping %
	•	Gives you a “LUT Quality Score”, a killer pro feature for the Studio tier.
