Perfect — color LUT analyzer 🎨

That actually has real sellable potential, but how you position it matters. Let’s break it down cleanly so you can decide whether to keep it free, freemium, or premium:

⸻

🎬 What a “Color LUT Analyzer” can be

If you’re just visualizing LUT data — e.g., displaying color curves, before/after previews, cube-to-3D graph — it’s cool but common.

If you enhance it, though — meaning:
	•	It detects contrast shifts, saturation bias, tone curve shape
	•	Shows delta-E (color error) metrics
	•	Auto-generates reports or visual heatmaps
	•	Lets users compare two LUTs side by side
	•	Optimizes or cleans LUTs (fix banding, clipping)
	•	or even applies LUTs to sample videos/images

👉 Then you’ve entered pro-tier territory — something content creators, LUT designers, and colorists would pay for.

⸻

💡 How to structure it

Here’s a realistic breakdown:

Version	Access	Description
Free (Replit/Web)	Public	Upload .cube LUT, see a 3D cube visualization + before/after preview
Pro / Paid Tier	$5–$15 license or subscription	Add advanced analysis: LUT comparison, tone mapping graphs, deltaE color difference, export report, AI-based correction
Studio Tier (Later)	$25–$50	Batch analyze multiple LUTs, export to CSV/JSON, plugin integration with DaVinci Resolve / Premiere

You can keep your Replit demo free and use it as the “public playground,” then link to the pro version (hosted separately or behind a Gumroad/Stripe paywall).

⸻

🧠 Marketing angle that sells

Don’t market it as “a tool to view LUTs.”
Market it as:

“A color scientist in your browser — analyze, compare, and optimize LUTs before they hit your footage.”

That line alone attracts cinematographers, LUT creators, and YouTubers who release cinematic packs.

⸻

🔗 Platforms that fit
	•	Gumroad — sell the enhanced version as a downloadable or hosted web app.
	•	Replit Deployments — host your demo or even full app behind authentication.
	•	ProductHunt / IndieHackers — great for exposure if it’s web-based.
	•	DaVinci Resolve communities — they love LUT tools if you can show side-by-side grading improvements.

⸻

🔥 Pro tip

If you later add an AI LUT classifier (e.g., “this LUT looks like teal-orange cinematic / Fuji Eterna style”), or a LUT suggestion based on uploaded footage, that becomes very sellable — like a mini “ColorGrading AI.”
